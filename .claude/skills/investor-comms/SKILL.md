# Investor Comms

Most founders go quiet after a "no" and silent between milestones. That's the
mistake. The investors who pass this round are often the leads of your next one —
*if* they watch you execute for six months. Warmth is built by consistency, not
charisma. This skill runs that consistency, plus the public comms around the raise.

## Prerequisite

Read `raise-context.md` for the facts, and the pipeline state from
`pipeline` to know who's on which track.

## Two parallel tracks

### Track 1 — Investors who said YES (or are live)
They're committed or close. Updates keep momentum and make them useful (intros,
hires, customers). Tone: collaborative, ask for specific help.

### Track 2 — Investors who said NO (or not now)
They passed but didn't dislike you. The goal is to make the next "yes" inevitable
by letting them watch you hit the milestones you said you would. Tone: confident,
no neediness, no re-pitching. Every update that lands a promised milestone quietly
rebuilds the case. When you out-execute the reason they passed, *they* re-engage.

## The monthly update (the core artefact)

Short, skimmable, sent like clockwork. Structure:

```
[Company] — [Month] Update
TL;DR: [one line — the month in a sentence]
📈 Metrics: [3–5 numbers vs. last month — the same metrics every time]
✅ Wins: [2–4, concrete]
🚧 Challenges: [1–2, stated honestly — this is what builds trust]
🙏 Asks: [specific — intros to X, hiring Y, anyone who knows Z]
What's next: [the next milestone]
```

The honest "challenges" line is the most important. Updates that are all wins read
as PR and get skipped. Naming a real challenge — and later showing you solved it —
is what makes investors trust the wins.

## Public communication around the raise

When the round closes (or for a planned announcement):
- **The announcement post** — what you raised, from whom, what it's for, the
  vision. Lead with the mission, not the money. Credit the leads.
- **Founder social posts** — the story behind the raise, written in the founder's
  voice (§13), not a press release. The "why now" and a thank-you to the people who
  helped.
- **Press note / one-paragraph boilerplate** — for any journalist or partner who
  asks. Consistent facts with the data room and deck.
Coordinate timing with the leads — never announce before they're ready.

## Procedure

1. Identify the track (yes/live vs. passed) or the public-comms ask.
2. For updates: pull the same recurring metrics each month so progress is legible;
   write the honest-challenge line; make the asks specific.
3. For public comms: lead with mission, match founder voice, keep facts reconciled
   with deck/model/room, and confirm timing with leads.
4. Keep a consistent cadence — the value is in showing up every month, on time.

## Principles

- **Consistency is the strategy.** A mediocre update sent every month beats a
  brilliant one sent twice. The signal is reliability.
- **Honesty in the challenges line.** Trust is built by what you admit, not what
  you boast. The challenge you name and then solve is your strongest proof.
- **A "no" is a "not yet with this information."** Out-execute the reason they
  passed, in writing, monthly, and the next round writes itself.
- **Mission before money in public.** The raise is a means; lead with what it's for.
